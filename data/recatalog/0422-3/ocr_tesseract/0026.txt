For giving his life for a fellow prisoner at Auschwitz in 1941, Pope John Paul II
canonized Maximilian Kolbe a saint. Even without that heroic act of self-sacrifice, he might
well have been canonized for his efforts as an evangelist. With a single-minded faith and zeal
that were by any definition extraordinary, Kolbe organized the largest publishing center in all
Poland. The impetus behind that achievement was an experience in 1917 in Rome, where he
was studying in Rome for the priesthood. Italian Freemasons in the streets of Rome celebrated
the 200th anniversary of modern Masonry with anti-clerical demonstrations that shocked Kolbe
deeply. Though only a twenty-three year old student, he founded the "Militia of the
Immaculata," an organization dedicated to the conversion of sinners, non-Catholics (including

Jews), but "especially Freemasons."

When the young Father Kolbe returned home, he determined to fight the influence of
Freemasonry in Poland by means of a popular Catholic press. In 1922 he began publishing the
"Knight of the Immaculata" (Rycerz Niepokalanej), an inexpensive monthly that by the end of
the next decade enjoyed the largest circulation of any periodical in Poland. The Vatican,
however, was not satisfied monthly periodicals or weekly newspapers, and in 1934 the Pope
demanded that the Polish bishops promote Catholic Action by promptly establishing a Catholic

daily newspaper. The Polish bishops had attempted to launch a Catholic daily earlier but failed.
