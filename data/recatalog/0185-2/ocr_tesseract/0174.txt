At the time I met him he was divorced and the father of twin
sons. The twin sons married twin sisters from Krakow, and when
Hitler deported people with Polish citizenship back to Poland, they
were deported, and, I must assume, later became victims of the
holocaust. I recall meeting the sons in Berlin.

This uncle visited us periodically, both to see his relatives
and to use our apartment and my aunt's apartment for art exhibits
and sales. He was the only relative who attended my mother's
funeral and was the person who notified me of her death. His
letters were in a beautiful script, in themselves pieces of art.
There were many stories of his Bohemian life style. When I was
still living in Birmingham, a friend, a Mr. Roubiszek was going to
Prague to visit his mother and arranged to meet Uncle Gustav. He
was most impressed with him and arranged for a teaching position in
the U.S. but my uncle still did not see the handwriting on the
wall, and, as a patriotic German, rejected the offer.

My Aunt Emma Früh was my favorite aunt. She was a widow living
in Berlin, also having lost a husband in World War I. Her daughter
Ilse, though older, and I got along wonderfully. This is the house
I stayed at when I visited Berlin. She assembled electrical
insulators for an electrical equipment factory, she did all of this
in her home. Though Rachel Sharon is related through my father's
side, Rachel was also related to this aunt, and it is in her house
that I first met Rachel. I believe the relationship is through a
Bitterman family, whose name comes up several times in the Birnbrey
family tree and which is also the maiden name of Rachel's mother.

Aunt Hannah was closest to my mother, both in relationship as
well as age. Aunt Hannah was never married and died at a very
young age. My mother took this death very hard, attesting to their
close relationship.

Aunt Fränzchen Jahn was the only sibling who was married out
of the faith. I made a courtesy call on them, met the family,
found them very nice and I had been forewarned about her fetish for
cleanliness and orderliness. I have not been able to find out
anything about the fate of this family.

Aunt Sophie Glaser and I did not get along well, or it could
have been because her daughter Lotte and I did not get along well.
I visited them briefly, they lived on the famous Alexanderplatz in
Berlin and she too was a widow. I always had the feeling that
Lotte was also able to get to Israel as she had attended a
Hachshara (Preparatory program for Aliyah) in Germany but I was
unsuccessful in finding her.
