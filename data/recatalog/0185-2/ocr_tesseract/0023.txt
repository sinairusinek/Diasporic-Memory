Hymie only owned 1/3 of the one and only Berman's store. Every once in a while you may
see documentaries on television concerning the fight for civil rights in Birmingham, and the
scene where the police had police dogs chasing the black protestors. In the background of
that scene you will generally get a brief glimpse of Berman's Store #3.

Next door to Berman's was the Bohemian Bakery which was the meeting place of the
Birmingham Jewish merchants. We had many arguments as a result of those meetings as
often Zaide Hymie would argue with me about certain facts, and his sole basis was that he
heard it differently at Bohemian, which made it gospel.

Zaide Hymie's life revolved around Knesseth Israel Synagogue where he attended the morning
minyan and the Y.M.H.A. where he went to swim and get a massage. Both Zaide and Bubbe
participated in the Chevra Kadisha.

The family was stricken with tragedy when their daughter Shirley (Shaine) became sick and
died. I believe this event had a lasting effect on Bubbe and Zaide from which they never
recovered. These were the days before medical insurance and the cost of medical treatment
including trips to Mayo Clinic wiped out their resources.

When World War II started, Zaide Hymie was anxious to do his part and volunteered for the
Sea Bees (nickname for "C.B.", the initials for the Navy's Construction Battalion) but he was
turned down. Their house became an open house for all of the Jewish soldiers who came to
Birmingham for the week-end and many lasting relationships were created. After all, with
three single daughters this made sense, and the subsequent marriage of Mushie was one of
those dividends, as Larry was a serviceman then stationed in Alabama.

When Sadie Courson's parents passed away, Sadie virtually became a daughter to Bubbe
Emma and the Kresses home was her second home. Likewise when Eunice was divorced,
she and Janet moved in with Bubbe Emma and Zaide Hymie, and I must say, Janet's
presence brought new life into the household.

In business, Zaide Hymie always enjoyed a reputation of being a person with the highest
integrity and he had the respect of his fellow merchants and suppliers. He was also
extremely modest and never wanted to appear as a big shot. He once asked me to type some
business letters for him and I, according to business usage, typed in a signature line referring
to him as "Partner" and he made me redo the letters to leave off any mention of his
proprietary interest in the store, and he told me: "just sign it "Kress".
