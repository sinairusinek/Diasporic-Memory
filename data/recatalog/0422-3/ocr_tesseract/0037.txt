Jewish death or could have helped and did not.

The bishops’ letter, though certainly historic, addressed only the Holocaust, not the
interwar years prior to it. Their letter did not acknowledge the fact that for years the church’s
leadership had, unwittingly at least, fostered attitudes that would only naturally view a Jewish
calamity with less than outrage. The Polish bishops wrote of failures of the Poles but not of the
church. They did not 0 the culpability of their forebears in the hierarchy, clergy,
Catholic Action, or Catholic press. If Jews during the Holocaust Jews were regarded as excluded
from what has been called the "universe of obligation" (Helen Fein), the conviction that Jews
were hostile to Catholic interests and Catholic culture constituted a major reason for that
exclusion. It was an era when the linkage of Judeo-Christian had not yet replaced that of Judeo-

communist.
