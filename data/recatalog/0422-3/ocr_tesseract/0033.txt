The Exception that Proved the Rule
Among the Catholic periodicals püblished in interwar Poland, Prad (Current) was by any
measure the most sympathetic to the problems faced by Polish Jewry. It explicitly criticized as
"demagogues" those writers who depicted Jews as willfully demoralizing Christian children. It
acknowledged that Jewish teachers were behaving in a most proper way, in every respect more
properly than some nominal Christian teachers. But even Prad questioned the wisdom of
government’s school integration policy, as simply escalating antagonism between Catholics and
Jews. Jewish teachers and administrators had a right to jobs in the public school system, but,
as non-Catholics, they could not help but represent something that conflicted with what children
were learning at home and in church. The conflict could not help but disturb the peace and
harmony required for learning. The problem was one of trying to integrate people from "two

entirely different cultures" in the same classrooms.‘
