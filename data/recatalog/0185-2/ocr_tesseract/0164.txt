THE BIRNBREY/LEVY FAMILY

It is ironical, when I began all of my search for family, I
thought that the Jacobsohn family was big and the Birnbrey family
relatively small. Additionally, I knew of no survivors in the
Birnbrey family, and now find that this family has a number of
survivors, though certainly not what it should have been had it not
been for the Holocaust. I am speaking in relative terms only.

It is also ironical that I know much less about the Birnbrey
Family than I do about the Jacobsohn Family, and I will try to
explain why.

I have been told that the Birnbrey family lived in Stettin,
Pommern, (Pomerania) for many generations. My grandparents died at
a relatively young age, my grandfather was Magnus Birnbrey (after
whom I received my Hebrew name Menashe), and my grandmother was
Auguste Levy. It appears that she had five brothers and sisters
and one of her sisters married a Gustav Gerson, and the Gerson's
made the largest addition in numbers to this family tree.

In researching the available documents from the Stettin
community I was unable to find our family names on those synagogue
memberships which were preserved in the archives of the Mormon
Church. The only item of interest and trivia I found is that
Mendelsohn personally conducted the premier of his composition
"Midsummer Night's Dream" in a concert hall which seemed to be
across the street of the family home on Schiitzengraben.

My father had two brothers, though until I met Rachel Sharon,
I was aware of only one.» One brother was Siegfried Birnbrey who
was Rachel's father and.who was married to Elisabet Bitterman,
whose family name appears ‘throughout the family tree. It is my
understanding that he had some type of textile factory next door to
the railroad station and when one exited the station, the name
"Birnbrey" was prominently seen. My father and his brother
Siegfried kept in touch through a very frequent exchange of letters
but otherwise, due to the distance, saw very little of one another.
