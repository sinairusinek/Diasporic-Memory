Uncle Albert Jacobsohn and his wife Käthe were not very close to their siblings. It was the
old complaint "he never writes or visits". It could be that he was a half brother or that his
life style was different. He ran a photo studio in Berlin and could not have been nicer to me
when I visited Berlin. He let me spend a lot of time helping in his dark room, which was
a real thrill for a 13 year old boy. He escaped to France, then escaped to Vichy France
where he was interned and after the war he lived in Marseilles. He and his wife both passed
away in France in the late 40s or early 50s.

As I pointed out, three of my mother's brothers were killed in World War I. One of these was
Emil Jacobsohn the father of Rosa and Alice, who was killed on August 3, 1917 near a town
by the name of Laon. Emil's wife, Jette, was the sister of Georg Fuchs who, because he was
my G-dfather, became my closest relative. All of us lived in the same apartment building
in Dortmund on Friedrich St. 13. When my mother's daughter Ruth was suffering from
malnutrition during the World War I, my aunt invited my mother, who was then living in
Frankfurt, to live with her in Dortmund because she, my aunt, had a cow and thereby could
provide milk for the child. However the child did die at a young age, I think age 2 or 3 and
was buried in Dortmund. The grave of my Uncle Emil is still in tact at the Ostfriedhof in
Dortmund (Jiidischer Teil Feld 14 e No. 250/251), however the field that my sister was
buried in, only a few yards away, on the same cemetery, has been flattened as a result of the
bombings of Dortmund during the war.

Alfred Jacobsohn was killed in World War I, and he was married to Rosel, who was a sister
of Martha Strauss. Martha Strauss was the mother of Walter and Henry Strauss, now living
in Atlanta. Aunt Rosel remarried and also lived in Dortmund and we did maintain some
relationship with her. When I left for the U.S. she made sure that I would contact her
nephew Walter, who was then living in Bluefield, W.Va.

My mother used to speak about a relative who went to Israel in the early 20s or thereabouts,
who, according to my mother, was the first "Stone Printer" in Haifa, Israel. I always thought
that this was some type of printing process, though it could also mean someone who inscribed
tomb stones. At any rate | have never figured out who this individual or family might be.

On this basis I have recorded ten brothers and sisters and | simply have no recall on who
was #11.

Thus, at this writing, and as far as I know, Alice Heiser and I, my children and
grandchildren are the sole survivors of the Jacobsohn family.
