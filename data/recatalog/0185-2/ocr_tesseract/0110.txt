PREFACE

This is an age when everyone searches for his or her roots. Some of it had its beginnings
when the popular book "Roots" was on every book shelf, some perhaps because we are now
studying sophisticated theories about genetics 610. and some of us are simply searching for
"connections".

Some families feel they have more relatives than they care to deal with, and some, like
myself, felt terribly alone following the destruction of most of my relatives in the Nazi
Holocaust.

A strange phenomena occurred in my search for roots. When World War II ended, I had
been in touch with two of my first cousins whom I knew had settled in Palestine, Rosa Blum
and Alice Heiser. They were the children of my mother's brother Emil Jacobsohn who was
killed in World War I. I knew of no survivors on my father's side of the family and thought
of myself as the sole survivor on that side.

This aloneness was difficult to comprehend as my mother was one of eleven siblings and my
father was one of three brothers.

In the back of my mind I recalled that my cousin Rosel Birnbrey (Rachel Sharon) went to
a Hachshara, a preparatory program for immigration to Palestine (Israel). Following the end
of World War II and over a period of two years I placed ads on Kol Yisrael, the Israel radio
network, which had a program to help people find and reunite with relatives. After the third
such announcement, someone who knew Rachel heard the announcement, contacted her, and
she sent me a telegram advising me that she was very much aliye, and the rest is history.

Now the story completely turns around. My cousins Rosa and Alice were childless, and lam
the only survivor on my mother's side of the family, and through Rachel, who shared my
quest for roots, we discovered that there were many survivors on my father's side of the
family. There are two slight caveats to this, one, of course my children and grandchildren
are "survivors" of both sides of the family, but for this epitaph I was only counting up to my
generation. The other caveat is that I did find one of my mother's brothers after World War
II, this was her half brother Albert Jacobsohn who was interned by the Vichy French who
survived, lived and died in France, but who also was childless.
