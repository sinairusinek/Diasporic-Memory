From the standpoint of the Catholic church leadership at least, the "Jewish question" in

interwar Poland was cultural rather than economic. Certainly anti-Jewish feelings among
average Poles were heightened by poverty, joblessness, and major population shifts from
agriculture to the urban industrial work force. But incidents of anti-Jewish violence escalated
not at the depths of the depression but in the period of gradual recovery, after the death of
Marshall Pitsudski in 1935. His passing plainly provided the National Democrats freer rein in
their nationalizing campaign, aided and abetted, however, by the clergy and the Catholic press,

for whom "national" meant Catholic and Jewish meant secular.
