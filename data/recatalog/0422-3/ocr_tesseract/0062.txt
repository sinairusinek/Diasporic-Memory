"Antisemitism"

Medieval antisemitism was rationalized by theological ideas. Jews were
seen as evil, killers of Christ, perpetrators of ritual murder and violaters of Christian
morals. In the 18th and 19th Century, antisemitism was rationalized by pseudo-
science. Jewish biology, not Jewish morality, justified the persecution of the Jews.
Polish antisemitism was more medieval than scientific. In Poland, the hatred of

Jews was nurtured by the mythology of Jewish malevolence.

Christian doctrine provided the ideological element of antisemitism in
Europe. Religious myths stirred fear of Jews. The ethical teachings of the Church
promoted disdain for the Jews. Catholic emphasis upon obedience to priests as
the shepherds of the flock, was a significant factor in promoting antisemitism.
