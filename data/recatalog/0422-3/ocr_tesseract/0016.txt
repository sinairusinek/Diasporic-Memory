As noted above, Jews in the medieval construct of European society were allowed to
reside in Christian lands to attest to their punishment and thereby the truth of Christianity. That
testimony required their being kept.in a condition of subservience. With the break-up of the

medieval hierarchies, however, and the social leveling, rep d by Fr mnry and

advanced by the political revolutions of the 19th century, the condition of European Jews
improved to the extent that the old metaphors were no longer appropriate. Jews were no longer
subservient outsiders. In the heart of what once was Christendom, Jews became identified as
subversives, joined with the archenemies of the Catholic church, the Freemasons, in a

conspiracy to undermine Christian society and culture as agents of a liberal secular culture.

Ignoring papal condemnations of Freemasonry made in 1738 and 1751, Catholics in both

Europe and the American colonies joined Masonic lodges right up to 1797.16 In that year,

however, Abbé Augustin Barruel published a four volume work, Memoires pour servir d’histoire
du Jacobinisme, in which he ascribed the French Revolution, especially its excesses to a Masonic
plot to overthrow Christian civilization. Scholars today recognize the falsity of that accusation.

were to be found among the victims of the revolution rather than among its agents. But by the
