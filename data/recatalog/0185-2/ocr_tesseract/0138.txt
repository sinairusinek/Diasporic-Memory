Naturally when World War I ended, and the soldiers returned home, coupled with the fact that
Germany no longer was allowed to have a war industry, which was followed by a terrible depression,
these Jewish people were the first to lose their jobs. This explains how so many Eastern European
Jews came to live in Germany and their ensuing state of poverty.

Likewise, a number of Germans were living in Poland on land which was ceded to Poland following
World War I. Among this group was also a large number of German Jews who had maintained their
German citizenship but now resided in Poland. The Nazi regime decreed that the Germans living
in Poland either had to return to Germany or lose their citizenship and thus become "Stateless" and
at the same time ordered those Jews residing in Germany who had Polish citizenship should forcibly
be returned to Poland.

The government began to round up these Polish Jews for transportation to Poland. When they
reached the Polish border, Poland refused to accept them, and some were forcible driven across the
border and with neither country wanting them, they were forced to live under inhumane conditions.

One of those families was a family named Grynszpan who had a son, Hershel Grynszpan, living in
Paris. Hershel was born in Hanover, Germany into a family of Polish Jews. When they wrote him
about their terrible plight, he became so incensed that he decided to do something and got a gun and
went to the German Embassy in Paris with the intention of killing the German Ambassador. Instead
he shot a 2nd Secretary, Ernst vom Rath, thinking he was killing the ambassador. This took place
around November 7.

At the same time the Nazi big wigs were holding a meeting in Munich to commemorate their aborted
1923 Putsch. On November 9, word was received that Vom Rath had died from the wounds
sustained and the Nazis were ready for revenge or perhaps had just been waiting for an excuse to
begin the process to eliminate and dehumanize the Jews.

Thus the action for Kristallnacht was hatched and communicated all over Germany. The Nz
still intent on not creating a worse world image than necessary, ordered the perpetrators not to wear
their uniforms so that no one could identify them either as Storm Troopers or members of the SS.

Store windows of Jewish owned stores were smashed, their goods looted, nearly all synagogues were
burned while the fire departments only concerned themselves with the fires not spreading to
neighboring buildings, and about 30,000 Jews were arrested and deported to concentration camps.

My knowledge of my father's involvement is very meager and sketchy. The only thing I know is that
something happened to him that night, that he was left in terrible physical shape and that my mother
was unable to have him admitted to any hospital or first aid station for required emergency treatment
because he was Jewish. About 9 or 10 weeks later a Catholic hospital in one of the suburbs of
Dortmund, Hörde, admitted him, but too late to provide a cure and he died on February 2, 1939
which was the 12th day of Shevat. I only know that the period between November 9 and February
2 was sheer nightmare, but because of the strict German censorship, my mother was unable to
describe these events in more detail.
