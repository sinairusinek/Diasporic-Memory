At the time I met him he was divorced and the father of twin sons. The twin sons married
twin sisters from Cracow, and when the Nazis deported people of Polish citizenship back to
Poland, they too were deported, and, I must assume, later became victims of the Holocaust.
I recall meeting the sons in Berlin.

This uncle visited us periodically, both to see his relatives and to use our apartment and my
aunt's apartment for art exhibits and sales. He was the only relative who attended my
mother's funeral and was the person who notified me of her death. His letters were in a
beautiful script and in themselves pieces of art. There were many stories of his Bohemian
life style. When I was still living in Birmingham, a friend, a Mr. Roubiszek, was going to
Prague to visit his mother and arranged to meet Uncle Gustav. He was most impressed with
him and arranged for a teaching position in the U.S. but my uncle still did not see the
handwriting on the wall, and, as a patriotic German, rejected the offer. Uncle Gustav was
born in 1878 and on February 19, 1943 he was on the 29th Transport to Auschwitz where
he was killed.

My Aunt Emma Früh was my favorite aunt. She was a widow living in Berlin, also having
lost a husband in World War I. Her daughter Ilse, though older, and I got along wonderfully.
This is the house I stayed at when I visited Berlin. She assembled electrical insulators in
her home for an electrical equipment factory. Though Rachel Sharon is related on my
father's side, Rachel was also related to this aunt, and it was in this house that I first met
Rachel. I believe the relationship is through a Bitterman family, whose name comes up
several times in the Birnbrey family tree and which was also the maiden name of Rachel's
mother. Aunt Emma was born in 188] and on October 27, 1941 she was transported on the
3rd Transport to the Ghetto Lodz.

Aunt Hannah was closest to my mother, both in relationship as well as age. Aunt Hannah
was never married and died at a very young age. My mother took her death very, hard,
attesting to their close relationship.

Aunt Fränzchen Jahn was the only sibling who was married out of the faith. I made a
courtesy call on them, met the family, found them to be very nice and I had been forewartied
about her fetish for cleanliness and orderliness. I have not been ableo find out anything
about the fate of this family. :

Aunt Sophie Glaser and I did not get along well, or it could have been because her daughter
Lotte and I did not get along well. I visited them briefly, they lived on the famous
Alexanderplatz in Berlin and she too was a widow. I always had the feeling that Lotte was
also able to get to Israel as she had attended a Hachshara (Preparatory program for Aliyah)
