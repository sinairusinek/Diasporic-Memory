Monsignor Stanislaw Trzeciak, self-styled expert on Jewish matters and a seasoned champion
of Catholic tradition, no attack on the proposed marriage legislation was more effective than to
label it Jewish. Basing himself on the concession the Talmud makes for divorce, Tzeciak
painted the authors of the legislative reform as either Jewish themselves or else inspired by a
Jewish spirit. Introducing civil marriage and divorce could only undermine Catholic family life
in Poland. Under the pretext of liberality, Jews were "imparting a spirit of decay into Christian

nations and societies, intentionally striving to destroy their culture, tradition, and faith. "29

Another "threat" Jews posed to Catholic culture was to be found in Poland’s public
schools. In 1929 Pope Pius XI had issued an encyclical on Christian education. In it he insisted
that the state had the responsibility of providing Catholic parents a religious education for their
children, taught by Catholic teachers and supervised by the bishops.*° In interwar Poland the
public schools allowed for religious instruction as part of the curriculum but were open to all
students of all nationalities and religions. Such "mixed schools" were tolerated by the Polish
bishops, so long as segregated neighborhoods and government policy resulted de facto in
separate schools. But the situation changed drastically, when the government began a program
of deliberate integration, mixing Catholic pupils with Jewish pupils in classes taught by Jewish

teachers.
