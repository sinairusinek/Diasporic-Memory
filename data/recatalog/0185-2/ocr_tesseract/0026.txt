There were many stories about his trips to Cuba to bring food and clothing to his relatives
and his trips to the North during the War to get merchandise for the store. My favorite story

is the following:

Zaide had gone to Baltimore to buy some shirts for the store. You have to remember that
during World War II most merchandise was scarce on account of goods being used in the war
effort. The manufacturer's warehouse was empty and he was told that there simply were no
shirts available. While sitting in the owner's office he spotted a photograph of a soldier on
the desk and asked if this soldier was named "so and so". The owner was shocked and asked
him how did he know the name. Zaid told him that the boy had been his guest on the yom
tovim. Needless to say, suddenly there were more shirts than Berman's Store # 3 had
intended to buy and Zaide Hymie returned to Birmingham like a hero. It was that difficult
to find goods during the war and if a store had goods, the customers were ready. This points
out how acts of TUT (good deeds) generally benefit both parties.

When Zaide Hymie sold his share in the business in order to retire, the family moved to
Atlanta, which, it is fair to state, brought a whole new dimension to their life style. First of
all they had family here, children and grandchildren, and second the affiliation and proximity
to Beth Jacob enabled Zaid not only to become Shomer Shabbath but gave him a whole new
religious experience involving learning and new friendships. Bubbe Emma was content with
attending Shule and her Tuesday morning class at Beth Jacob and help in mending books and
her new found friend Mrs. Skolnik, whom I referred to as the "opstaireske". I had the feeling
that they never once looked back to Birmingham.

Zaide Hymie had several brothers and sisters. His oldest brother was Feivel who was the
father of our Cuban cousins. 1 think his sister Rachel Sopher was next and she also was the
last survivor of that generation. She and her husband Sam ran a tiny piece good store on
Bathgate Avenue in the Bronx. There was a brother Willie, or Velvel, who was never
married. For some reason he would not speak to his siblings but was always nice to his
nieces and nephews. No one ever knew for sure why he refused to speak to his siblings.
The youngest sister was Ethel Goldfarb who was married to Morris Goldfarb (Meshe Lazer).
He was a kosher butcher in the Bronx. Our children made fun of his Yiddish name, referring
to him as Meshe Lazy. Both Faivel and Rachel were extremely religious, and though the
Goldfarbs operated a kosher butcher shop, they were not religious.

We had reservations to go to Israel when Zaide Hymie passed away. The reservations, as it
turned out, were for a few hours following the end of the Shiva period and we were therefore
able to arrive in Israel in time for Yogi's birth and the name Yochai is after Chaim Yitzchak
ben Moshe?"T. Bubbe Emma passed away a couple of years later and Emma Stein is named
after Temme bat Yaakov Shlomo Hacohen?"T.
