Rosa Jacobsohn Blum

In Memoriam

This is written for the younger generation, for those who did not know the Rosa I knew, before she
suffered and before she became dependent on other people for all of her physical needs.

We mourn the loss of one who had an unusual will to live, unequalled and certainly difficult to
understand by those who give up on life with much less of a malady.

Many of you have only known the Rosa who couldn't see and the Rosa who couldn't walk, and who
in spite of all of this, was always the Rosa who could comprehend and who could understand, and
even some times, much to her credit, could complain.

Those of my generation, knew an entirely different Rosa, a Rosa who contributed to life, a Rosa who
always had an open house, a Rosa who loved life and a Rosa who was a giver of care, not a receiver
of care.

Rosa was born in 1908 and at age 9 word was received that her father, Emil Jacobsohn was killed
in World War I (August 3, 1917). Her parents were business partners with a Martin Goldschmidt in
a business known as Goldschmidt und Jacobsohn on RheinischerstraBe in Dortmund.

As soon as Rosa was old enough, she took the family's place in that business and virtually became
the head of her family.

My mother also lost a husband in World War I and she was left with a baby who was suffering from
malnutrition. Rosa's mother had a cow which provided milk, and she invited my mother and her baby
to live with her and thus began a beautiful bond between the two sister-in-laws which continued with
the two families. Unfortunately the baby died shortly thereafter.

For many years all of us resided in the same apartment building which was also the same building

in which Gila and Natan's grandmother, great grandmother and family resided.

A Nazi landlord made living there unbearable, we moved out and shortly thereafter Rosa and Alice
made Aliyah to Israel to be followed by their mother, who remained in Dortmund long enough to
participate in my Bar Mitzvah.

World War II began, and contact between families was most difficult and we really did not re-
establish regular contact until the war was over.
