While Poland had become identified with refuge for Jews, for Roman Catholics the
metaphor was quite disparate. Poland was the bastion or bulwark of western Christendom
(antemurale christianitatis).5 Between the years 1000 and 1939, from both inside and outside
Poland, any number of allusions can be found illustrating the Catholic conviction that (along
with neighboring Hungary) Poland was the last outpost of western civilization. In its earliest
centuries it held the line against Prussian and Lithuanian pagans, later against Islamic Turks and
schismatic Russians, and finally in the 20th century against Soviet communism. Its record of
resistance against these assaults from the East earned it the title, "Polonia semper fidelis." A
staunchly Catholic Poland ever faithful to Rome was obviously at odds, however, with a later
slogan that identified Poland as a “haven of tolerance" not only for Jews but for dissenters of
all stripes.
