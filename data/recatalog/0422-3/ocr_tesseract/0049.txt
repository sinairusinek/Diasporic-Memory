From an ontological point of view, the sources of hostility to Jews are
limited. Animosity towards Jews can be based upon experiences or can be the
result of mythology. The long history of hatred of the Jews has been nurtured by
both sources. The two thousand years of the Diaspora made the Jews a target
of xenophobia. The dislike of strangers is nearly universal and, in a certain sense,
"normal". Being a stranger is real, it can be observed. Babies cry when picked
up by strangers, dogs bark when a stranger enters the village. Xenophobia is a
part of human condition. The dislike of the Jews in ancient Athens, like the
hostility to the Irish in Boston and New York at the turn of this century, was
experiential. A Hellenized Jew, an Americanized Irishman, could be integrated into
the society. Xenophobia can be overcome by assimilation.
