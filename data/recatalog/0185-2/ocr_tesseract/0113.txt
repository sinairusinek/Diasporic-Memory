THE JACOBSOHN FAMILY

Virtually all I know about my mother's family are my memories of the stories she told me.
We lived far away from the family (for pre-war German standards) and saw very little of them

with few exceptions.

As far as I knew, the family had lived in Pommern (Pomerania) for many generations. My
grandfather, Theodor, died in 1937 in a home for the aged in Berlin. He and his second wife
were in separate hospitals, and unbeknown to the other, died on the very same day. They
lie buried in twin graves on the WeiBensee Jewish Cemetery in Berlin. My grandfather was
ninety years old, and considering that he had been a photographer all of his life, one has to
assume that he was among the pioneers in that profession.

He was first married to Henriette, after whom I was named. They had ten children and lived
in Falkenburg, Pommern (now Zlocieniec, Poland), and later moved to Kallies, Pommern (now
Kalisz, Poland). When my grandmother died, my grandfather remarried and had one more
child, Albert Jacobsohn. (Falkenburg is 94 KM ENE of Stettin and Kallies is 69 KM SW

of Gdansk). (Stettin is now known as Szczecin).

My grandfather served in the Prussian Army, and, I understand, that he had officer rank,
which was a rarity for Jews. He served in the War of 1866 against Austria and again in the
Franco-Prussian War of 1870/71.

Three of his sons were killed in World War I (thus my mother lost three brothers, a husband
and a child in "The War to End all Wars"!).

Legend has it, that I visited my grandparents at age one, not yet able to walk, my grandfather
handed me a walking cane and off I went and walked from then on. This was also the one
and only time I saw him.

All of my mother's siblings, with the sole exception of my mother and Rosa and Alice's father,
settled in Berlin. After my bar mitzvah in 1936 I visited Berlin and met all of the surviving
brothers and sisters and their families.

The most colorful relative was my Uncle Gustav. He was an artist and had spent his life
painting in Rothenburg and in the Shtetls of Poland painting Jewish scenes. Before World
War I he was reputed to have painted portraits of the Kaiser's children for the Kaiser. My
mother considered him a famous artist, to this date I have been unable to find his name in
any books, catalogues and/or lists of artists. There also was talk that he had taught at the
