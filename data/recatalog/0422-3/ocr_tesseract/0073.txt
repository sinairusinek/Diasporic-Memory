B: General
"Antisemitism"

Jesus and his initial followers were part of the Judaic religion. The
establishment of a new religion apart from Judaism occurred after the death of
Jesus. Anti-Judaic beliefs as part of Christian creed developed even later. Anti-
Jewish sentiments, as an expression of xenophobia, pre-existed anti-Judaic views
of the early church fathers.

Christian anti-Judaism, to the extent that it was rational, gave the Jews a
way out—they could convert. Xenophobic anti-Judaism, to the extent that it was
"normal" provided the option of assimilation. Mythical, demonic anti-Judaism
viewed Jews as being beyond redemption. Christianity, starting in the third
century, progressively demonized the Jews.
