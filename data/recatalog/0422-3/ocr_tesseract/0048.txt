IV. The post-Holocaust anti-Judaism

The pre-Christian anti-Judaism and the post-Holocaust anti-Judaism are part
of the antagonism to strangers. The Christian anti-Judaism and antisemitism are
specific to the Jewish-Christian encounter.

A meeting of two cultures results in conflict. The Jews and Judaism were
disdained in the Hellenic world. In Athens, polytheism was the religion and
Prometheus was the human ideal. In Jerusalem, one abstract entity was
worshipped and Job was the role model. Athens nurtured philosophy, i.e. secular
knowledge; Plato and Aristotle were revered. Jerusalem was dominated by
prophets, i.e. God's messengers. Conflict was inevitable between Athens and

Jerusalem. It took an unusual citizen of either city to find a visitor from the other
