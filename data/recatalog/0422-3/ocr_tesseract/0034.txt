Father Antoni Szymafiski, a professor of ethics at the University of Lublin. Long before Hitler’s

among Catholic periodicals in encouraging economic cooperation between Catholics and Jews.
Poland was suffering economically not for want of natural or human resources but for want of
peaceful coexistence among its various communities. Individuals were sowing seeds of distrust
and dividing people who, though living close to each other, really did not know one another.

With sentiments like these characteristic of Odrodzenie, it was little wonder that one nationalist
writer declared that this “originally Catholic" and "once influential" organization had "come

under the influence of Masons and Jews."44

Father Szymariski believed that Prad’s Catholic readers should know more about Polish
Jewry, or, as he put it, the "Polish citizens of the Mosaic religion." He described at length
the religious, political, and social diversity of Jews, and criticized those "wide circles" that held
Jews accountable for the bolshevik revolution. He did not deny that Jews were among the
