To add insult to injury, when the various German insurance companies complained to the government
about their inability to pay the huge financial claims for losses of Jewish property ete. the government

instead fined the Jews 1 Billion Marks to pay for the damage.

My father was buried in an unmarked grave at the Jewish part of the Main Cemetery (Field 2 B-12)
in Dortmund, and only because of the description given me by my Aunt Hilde and the maps and
records of the cemetery was I able to locate the grave in 1972 and following the dictates of halacha,
I erected a matseva, so that the world knows that he lived and died. (My mother is buried in the
next row).

To add to the Kristallnacht episode, in 1972, when I visited Dortmund, I noted that a beautiful opera
house graced the ground where our synagogue had stood. There were no markers to indicate what
was. In my conversation with the mayor of Dortmund I mentioned this "oversight" and when I
revisited a year later, yes, there was a marker. This is what we mean that we have to be witnesses
to history and continue to remind the world so that these martyrs and the atrocities will not be
forgotten.

In memory of Edmund (Ezra ben Menashe) Birnbrey, born October 1, 1888 in Stettin Germany, died
s death, February 2, 1939 in Dortmund, Germany.
