"Antisemitism"

European antisemitism, particularly in the eastern European countries, has
to be seen in the social-political context. In European societies, the source of
power was an authority—be it divine or secular. In an authoritarian society,
significant segments of the population are oppressed and angry. Any challenge
to prevailing views constitutes rebellion, is angerous, and suppressed.
Disagreement is perceived as subversion; loyal opposition is vewed as a
contradiction in terms. Whoever questions ecclesiastical authority is abeled a

heretic. Those who oppose secular authority are accused of treason.

An authoritarian society had an insatiable need for scapegoats. The Jews

were ideal targets of scapegoating. They were powerless, viewed as inferior, and
they were stubbornly defiant to ecclesiastical authority.

answers. Why is there so much evil? Why do societal arrangements cause
