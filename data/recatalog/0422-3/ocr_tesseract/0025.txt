Pope Pius XI is remembered favorably for his encyclicals condemning communism and
Nazi racism. He was just as strong, however, in his criticism of the separation of church and
state, which he saw as inevitably leading to communist and Nazi tyranny. Government leaders,
he wrote, were bound to give public honor to Christ and to his empire over all nations. It was
impious anti-clericalism for governments to avoid mentioning his name and an ignominy for the
Catholic church to be placed on the same level as "false religions."29 The church, wrote Pius
XI in his first encyclical, was the teacher and guide not only of individuals but of societies and
nations. He promised a "holy battle" to vindicate the church’s rights over education.°?

Promoting and defending those rights was one of the important tasks of Catholic Action.

Catholic Action had its origins with Pope Leo XIII’s opposition to separation of church
and state and Pope Pius X’s program to restore Catholic culture. But it was Pius XI who
became the "Pope of Catholic Action" and gave it its classical definition: the cooperation of the
laity in the apostolate of the hierarchy. Promoting it frequently in his letters and addressees,
Pius XI insisted that Catholic Action remain above partisan politics. But its purpose was
precisely to make lay Catholics the agents of the pope’s and bishops’ influence on social and
political life. Liberalism, wrote Pius XI, had prepared the way for communism, and he
