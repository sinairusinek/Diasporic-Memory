When I met Rachel in Berlin she let me in on a family secret.
One day when she came home from school, she related to her parents
that she and the other children had made fun of a crippled man on
the way home. In describing the incident and the person, her
parents explained to her that this person was a brother of her
father (and my father). Apparently this person was sort of a
black sheep in the family, though I still do not know the reason.
About a year after my meeting with Rachel we were notified of his
death and asked to contribute funds to pay for the funeral. These
two incidents are my only knowledge of this third Birnbrey brother.

A year before I left for the U.S. we were called to the
Police Station. It appeared that we had inherited some money from
a relative in San Francisco, we had to sign some papers and the
funds were then confiscated by the Germans. This is one connection
I never was able to figure out, though my father did speak about
San Francisco relatives. Rachel told me that one of my
grandfather's sisters married a man named Bernstein who lived in
San Francisco and that she tried to contact him but that he was
very rude to her.

My father moved from Stettin to Stuttgart where he was
employed by an insurance company. When World War I broke out he
served in the Württemberg Army, was wounded, earned the Iron Cross
and somehow met my mother who was then a widow. He spent his
entire life keeping up with many army buddies both by
correspondence and frequent visits. None of these buddies were
Jewish and most of them continued some type of relationship during
the Hitler period.

The largest part of this family tree is attributed to Alfons
Gerson who did a fantastic job in putting together the Gerson
family tree and whose daughter coincidentally now lives in
Dortmund. :

One of my uncle Siegfried's daughters died of pneumonia at
age 14 while I still lived in Dortmund, one daughter Rachel made
aliyah to Israel and the rest of the family were victims of the
Holocaust. *

Most surviving relatives of this family live in Israel, I met
one in Buenos Aires and some live in the U.S. The name "Birnbrey",
to the best of my knowledge and belief, survives only through
myself and my children and children's children.

The known names of those. who died as the result of the
Holocaust, are shown on a separate list. However, since my father,
Edmund Birnbrey, was killed as the result of Kristallnacht (Crystal
Night), he probably was the first victim of the holocaust of the
family.
