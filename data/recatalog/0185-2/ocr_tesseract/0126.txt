In 1948, during the Siege of Yerusholayim, another bond was forged. Frieda Macarov was pregnant
with her first child, Varda, and she was flown out of Yerusholayim to Tel Aviv in order to give birth
at Hadassah Hospital in Tel Aviv. Of course, Rosa's home was opened and Dave and Frieda have
never forgotten the Hachnassath Orchim extended to them, and just as I, Rosa became a part of the
Macarov Family.

When Rosa married Natan Blum, we were privileged to receive the gift of a relative whom everybody
loved. Their modest home became the center of family get-togethers, their tiny kitchen produced
meals which could not be equalled in the finest of kitchens, and when you were served it was with
the joy of giving and love and their home was filled with laughter.

In my entire life I have never met a man who was more proud of every little possession, and who
enjoyed sharing these possessions with others, than Natan Blum.

The way Natan took care of Rosa was above and beyond the obligations of a spouse and obviously his
care and concern made a tremendous impression on the next man in Rosa's life, Meir Alaloof.

What Meir did for Rosa, can only be done for those who left this kind of a mark and while it certainly
was a tribute to Meir, and to whom I shall be forever grateful, it was also a tribute to the mark both
Natan and Rosa must have made on Meir. His care, love and tremendous giving of his time and
energies is something that I will never forget, and I hope and pray that you will never forget. Meir
was a model for all of us and as we mourn Rosa and as we mourned Natan, we can only be grateful
and thankful that a Meir appeared, thus out of everything bad and sad, something beautiful occurred.

It is fitting that Rosa's death occurred in the month of Elul when a Jew prepares for his or her return
to G-d, we pray that Rosa has returned to her well deserved peace and end of her suffering and may
Alice find comfort by recalling the beautiful years of her Rosa and may Meir, a true Lamed Vavnik,
