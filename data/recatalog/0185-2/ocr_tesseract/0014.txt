The Jewish community building and school were closed and we only saw the outside.

Our guide then gave us a "City Tour" and I was amazed to see the size and beauty of the
old castle. To the credit of the eity, this castle is being used for all sort of cultural events
and it has a steady program of operas, operettas and concerts and an art school operates out
of there as well. All performances are free. And judging from the crowds, the performances
are well attended. Here also the people looked nicer and were better dressed. We had lunch
in the castle restaurant, and based on our kashrut compromises, we ordered a mushroom
omelette made out of dough instead of eggs which tasted wonderful. The cost of the meal for
the three of us came to over 200,000 Zlotys, which amounted to about $10.

The train station was very shabby and the only hardship on the train ride was the long
wait at the border for passport and custom inspection. The train ride itself, took 2% hours
out of Berlin.

The guide, Marek Karczewski, had us visit his home where his wife had baked us a
wonderful lemon cake. He was very educated, a specialist in the fishing industry, having
served in the Polish diplomatic service at various fishing rights conferences, but decided to
become an international ski instructor, tour guide and cab driver instead, to make a living.

Time did not allow us to visit the place of my mother's birth, Falkenburg. We may have
to save this for another visit, or perhaps one of my children may get the urge. Falkenburg

is about 90 km. from Stettin.
