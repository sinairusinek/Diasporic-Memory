It was another matter altogether, however, when in 1854 Eckert’s book appeared in
French. Jews were well represented in French Freemasonry, particularly with Adolphe
Cremieux, the President of the Alliance Israélite Universelle, serving at the same time as the
grand master of the Scottish Rite in France. In 1880 an obscure village priest, E.N. Chabauty,
charged that Jews were not only in league with Freemasons but in control of them.”° His book
became a primary source for Eduard Drumont’s 1886 best-seller, La france juive, the bulk of
which dealt with what Drumont called the "persecution" of Catholic France by Freemasons and
Protestants, both under the influence of Jews. There is some question about Drumont’s private
attitude toward Christianity, but he ‚presented himself as a Catholic writing for Catholics, and

his book was received warmly by the Catholic press and clergy.

The point at issue between liberal republicans and traditionalists was no less than the
definition of what it meant to be French. As such, the "Jewish question" figured intrinsically.
With more than some justification, French Catholics saw their church as the most important
institutional victim of the French Revolution. They placed the blame for its privation on
France’s Freemasons, the self-appointed champions of the revolution, and Jews, prominently
among its foremost beneficiaries. The notion of a Masonic-Jewish alliance played a conspicuous

role in the Dreyfus Affair (1894-1906), and by 1900 three different anti-Masonic organizations
